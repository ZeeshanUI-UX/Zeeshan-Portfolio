The Challenge

Support that keeps veterans in control.

Veterans, the peer advocates who support them and the community partners who deliver services all needed their own tools, but the work between them had to stay connected and private.

A veteran agrees before any referral is sent, recording needs its own consent, and each role sees only its own people and data.

The goal: three panels that share one shell and one vocabulary, with a crisis line visible on every screen.

The Process

Six roles, three journeys.

I mapped who does what: the Veteran registers, gets matched and follows a plan; the Advocate enrolls, trains, meets, plans and refers; the Partner accepts, serves and closes each referral with an outcome. Staff, command and supervisors work through those panels.

The Structure

One shell, three panels.

Every desktop screen follows the same skeleton: sidebar, top bar, crisis notice, hero card, three stat cards and a two-column body. Only the content and vocabulary change by role.

The Solution

Every row says what’s next.

Screens were rewritten to be short and plain: each row says what happened and “Next: …”. Statuses, consent and approvals are visible where the decision is made, and states cover empty, overdue, blocked and could-not-load.

The Outcome

Three things shaped
the design.

  1. 01

    Safety first

    A crisis notice on every screen, consent before every referral and separate consent for recording.

  2. 02

    One design language

    The approved Veteran panel set the style; Advocate and Partner follow the same shell and components.

  3. 03

    Wired prototypes

    Over 240 frames with numbered names and real links, so every button leads to a real screen.
